# Overview

Across disciplines, systems fail not because components stop working, but because **proportionality breaks**.

Energy scales faster than structure.  
Information grows faster than integration.  
Complexity accumulates faster than viability.

Traditional theories often address these failures *locally*.  
Systemic Admissibility reframes them *structurally*.

The framework asks a prior question:

> Under what conditions is a system allowed to persist, scale, or remain intelligible at all?

Admissibility is not optimization.  
It is **permission under constraint**.

This perspective applies uniformly across:
- Physical regimes (cosmology, thermodynamics, quantum/classical boundaries)
- Biological systems (aging, disease, regeneration)
- Cognitive and artificial systems (intelligence scaling, alignment)
- Socio-economic structures (markets, institutions, governance)

The framework does not explain *what happens*.  
It explains *when continuation is structurally allowed*.
