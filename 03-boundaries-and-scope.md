# Boundaries and Scope

This framework is intentionally bounded.

## What It Does
- Identifies conditions under which systems remain viable
- Explains why scaling fails across regimes
- Provides a unifying admissibility criterion

## What It Does Not Do
- Predict empirical outcomes
- Replace domain-specific theories
- Propose mechanisms or interventions
- Claim ontological primacy

## On Language and Naming

Terms such as *admissibility*, *constraint*, and *proportionality* are used deliberately to avoid overlap with existing frameworks centered on coherence, optimization, or equilibrium.

The framework positions itself as a **meta-structural condition**, not a competing theory.

## Admissibility as Boundary Signal

Breakdowns, paradoxes, and regime tensions are interpreted as **signals of violated admissibility**, not as anomalies to be patched.

Where admissibility fails, continuation becomes structurally impossible.
